First, the place was awesome, just as expected. At low tide, the beach really goes on forever.

To say the trip was a disappointement is an understatement. Issues encountered:

  1. Not enough power
  2. Terrible internet signal

It appears that, while we enjoy getting away, the beautiful environment, we also have our habits and our needs. It should not have been surprising that we wanted internet access, and found it difficult when the phones/Hotspot failed due to insufficent signal. Similarly, we appreciate some level of creature comfort and running out of battery power was an experience we are choosing to not repeat

Where does that leave us?

Internet

The internet thing is easily solvable with a Starlink. The van has an external connector for the Starlink Standard, which appears to terminate inside as a RJ45 connector. My understanding is that the dish's cable goes outside, the RJ45 inside goes in the router, in the van, and line voltage (120 VAC) goes to the router. Confusingly, the mobile van is equipped with a connector for the Starlink intended for a static permanent installation.

An alternative is the Starlink mini, That one is intended for nomads, highly portable and requiring less power. By default, it would plug into 120 VAC, but adapters exist to power it from a cigarette lighter plug (adapter has a 12 VDC-> 24 VDC adapter to avoid the low end of the range of acceptable voltage and reduce the current on the 15m cable.

The Starlink mini seems like the right choice for us.

Battery power

The power thing is not as easily solvable as the internet thing.

We do not know exactly how much charge was in the battery over the weekend. We do know we did not enough power. Was this because we started with a battery with a low charge level or because we used more power than the battery supports? Whatever the answer, we aborted our trip on Saturday to avoid going in damage zone in the battery (below 50%) on Sunday morning. Also, during the day on Saturday, we limited our power usage more than we are comfortable with.

Some information from Shawn, at Coachmen

  1. When the switch (Disconnect) is off, "the Truma" remains on AND is the only thing powered
  2. Confirmed the CO detector turns off, contradicting the information from Claude and Google AI
  3. "The truma" is unclear. Shawn pointed me to the control panel. On the control panel, I can turn off the heater + AC, but not the panel itself.
  4. It is possible that he meant that the heater remained on when we put the van in storage, with Disconnect in the OFF position. That sounds so broken, that you go in "don't use the battery" mode and the heater remains on. That would certainly explain the dead battery last week.
  5. Yet, that appears to be the only thing I can turn off. IF I am correct that the control panel cannot be turned off, then it is a "parasitic draw", small but existent. The same applies to the two Garmin screen, which are still on (albeit with white text on black background) when I turn off at the disconnect button.
  6. By design, it appears the expectation is that, while in storage, the van will receive power from either the solar or plugged in shore power (a 15 A circuit is sufficient here). Storing the van for weeks without a source of power is "not supported" (my words).

Conclusion(s)

  1. Internet: Go Starlink mini
  2. Battery: Appt at Courtnay RV Specialists on next Friday (Aug 28, 2026 8:00 AM) for parasitic draw investigation and evaluating options to switch to Lithium
  3. Lithium, installed in Courtenay, would likely be 460Ah (same 8D size) from Canbat, (the 300 Ah is $1,800; the 460 Ah is on special at $2,000). That would triple our available Ah, making any concerns go away, even with our comfortable (as opposed to rationed) power usage.
